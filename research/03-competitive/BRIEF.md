# Brief: 03 — Competitive Landscape

Read `research/README.md` first. It holds the thesis, the founder's decisions and the research standards, and those are binding.

## Goal
Map everyone who partly delivers the thesis: **self-custody/tokenized rails + an agentic/advisory layer + access products for £200k+ internationals**. Work out who is closest, where the white space is, and why the closest players haven't won (or whether they are winning).

## Categories to cover (verify each player from primary sources: their site, T&Cs, fee schedule, licences, filings, funding announcements)
1. **Neo-wealth / cross-border wealth for EM users:** Karta (the reference; analyse it in depth), and peers serving LATAM, Africa, India and Asia with US assets or credit. Find the peers; don't just rely on ones you know.
2. **Expat / international wealth managers and platforms:** e.g. offshore advisers, international private banks with low minimums, Interactive Brokers as the DIY default, expat-focused robo-advisers.
3. **Private banks and their thresholds:** entry minimums and what you get (Lombard credit, access to alternatives).
4. **Developed-market robo / neo-wealth:** e.g. Revolut wealth, Wealthfront, Betterment, Moneybox, Nutmeg, Scalable, Trade Republic. Only as far as they're relevant to the £200k+ international segment.
5. **Crypto-native wealth / credit / yield:** e.g. Coinbase, Kraken, Nexo, Ledn, Figure, plus wallets moving into "wealth".
6. **Tokenized RWA distribution to retail / HNW:** platforms giving non-US users tokenized equities, treasuries and private credit (e.g. Robinhood EU stock tokens, xStocks/Backed, Ondo Global Markets, Securitize). Verify current status, because this space has moved fast.
7. **Agentic / AI financial advisers:** startups and incumbents shipping AI agents that advise or act on users' money. Check what they can actually *execute* versus only chat about.

## For each player, capture
Target segment and geographies; core products; custody model (custodial / self-custody / hybrid); access products offered (credit, US assets, alternatives, yield); advice model (none / robo / human / AI); licences and regulators; minimums and fees; traction (users, AUM, revenue if public); funding (amount, investors, date); notable weaknesses (from reviews, enforcement actions, product gaps).

## Deliverable
`research/03-competitive/findings.md`, `sources.md` and `competitors.csv` (one row per player, the columns above). In `findings.md` include:
- A **positioning map** (described in text/table) on two axes: custody model × breadth of access products, with an advisory-layer overlay.
- The **5 closest competitors**, each with a deep dive, their moat, and how they'd respond to us.
- **Graveyard:** failed or pivoted players in this space and why they failed (e.g. crypto lenders in 2022, shut-down robo-advisers).
- **White space:** where nobody is, and whether that's because of opportunity or because it doesn't work.

## Out of scope
Segment sizing (01) and detailed regulatory analysis (05). Note anything relevant you find and move on.
